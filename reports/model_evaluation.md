## Setup

- Training set: `train_scaled.csv`, 8,000 rows, 271 failures. Test set: `test_scaled.csv`, 2,000 rows, 68 failures (Kiat's stratified 80/20 split, `random_state=42`).
- Inputs: `type` (L=0, M=1, H=2) and the five sensors, scaled with `ai4i_sensor_scaler.joblib`. IDs and the failure-mode labels (TWF, HDF, PWF, OSF, RNF) are excluded.
- Model and threshold chosen with 5-fold stratified cross-validation on the training set only. The test set was used once, for the final numbers below.
- The decision threshold maximises failure-class F1 on the out-of-fold training predictions.

## Notes for the report

- The majority-class baseline reaches about 96.6% accuracy while catching zero failures. This is why failure-class precision, recall and F1 are the headline metrics.
- Temperature difference and power are physical quantities, but the thresholds they relate to (HDF: difference < 8.6 K with speed < 1,380 rpm; PWF: power < 3,500 W or > 9,000 W) are the published **rules used to generate** the synthetic AI4I data. The model learning them is expected, not a discovery of real safety limits.
- The SMOTE file was not used for selection: its synthetic rows cannot be cross-validated without leakage, and ordinary SMOTE interpolates the categorical `type`. Class weighting / threshold tuning handle the imbalance instead. Its test result is listed above for completeness.
- Random failures (RNF, 0.1% of records) are random by construction and cannot be predicted.
- AI4I is synthetic and has no repair costs or downtime, so savings cannot be measured from it.
